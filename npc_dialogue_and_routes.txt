================================================================================
CHRONICLE OF THE LOST REALM — NPC DIALOGUE, FEELINGS & ROUTES
NOVEL-PACING AUTHORING FILE
================================================================================

PURPOSE
-------
This is the prose companion to story_systems_bible_EXPANDED.txt. The Bible
defines systems, flags, and event outcomes. This file defines how named people
feel, speak, conceal things, and change across long-form play.

The target is novel pacing: context before crisis; a person before a quest;
aftermath after consequence. A scene must not exist solely to deliver a choice.
It should leave the player with an image, an unanswered question, or a changed
feeling toward someone.

NON-NEGOTIABLE PACING RULES
---------------------------
1. Introduce a place before asking the player to save it. Use weather, work,
   smell, local custom, overheard talk, and one small ordinary problem.
2. Introduce an NPC in at least two beats: first an observable action, then a
   conversation. Do not open with their secret, biography, or quest request.
3. Between major event stages, write 2–4 breathing scenes: travel, a campfire,
   a letter, a meal, a rumor, an argument, or a quiet aftermath.
4. Every important NPC needs one mundane want and one private wound. The
   mundane want makes them believable; the wound creates story pressure.
5. Violence is an escalation, never filler. Gore must change the survivor,
   relationship, reputation, or world state. Do not use it only for shock.
6. Love, revenge, redemption, suspense, and drama are slow-burn patterns.
   No route may resolve on a first meeting or one perfect dialogue choice.
7. A choice should cost time, trust, safety, money, information, or a future
   opportunity. The player should feel what they gave up, not only what gained.
8. Never force a player to read a lore dump to proceed. Curiosity opens lore;
   urgency advances the plot; both remain valid play styles.
9. CHOICE REACTIONS ARE MANDATORY, NOT OPTIONAL. Added after a user report
   that reading the game felt like "skipping" and left them lost — the cause
   was found in the actual scene JSON, not a vague pacing problem: choices
   that represented different actions, different origins, or different stat
   -check outcomes were landing on the exact same next narrative with zero
   acknowledgment of what the player had actually just done. That is a bug,
   every time it happens, not an acceptable shortcut. Concretely:
   - If a stat check's success and its failure route to the same
     outcomeSuccessNode/outcomeFailNode, split them. A failed attempt must
     read differently from a skipped attempt, which must read differently
     from a clean success — even if the difference is one sentence.
   - If three or more choices converge on one hub scene (common at a
     chapter's gathering point), the hub's OPENING lines must react to what
     was actually done, not describe the same transition regardless of which
     choice was picked. Have each choice's effects set a shared
     `world_flags.<key>` to a distinct value, then give the hub scene a
     `narrativeVariant` for every value. A single generic paragraph standing
     in for a dozen different actions is exactly the bug this rule exists to
     stop.
   - The reaction does not need its own full scene or a dialogue exchange —
     one specific sentence, in the very next thing the player reads, is
     enough. It just has to exist, and it has to be about THIS choice, not
     filler ("word moves fast," "the road continues") that could follow any
     choice equally.
   - Self-test before shipping a branching scene: cover the choice list and
     read only the next paragraph the player would see. If you can't tell
     which choice was picked from that paragraph alone, the reaction is
     missing — go back and write it before moving on.

10. MINIMUM CONTENT DENSITY - PRIORITY RULE. Added after the user watched a
    playthrough skip from one meaningful choice almost straight into a
    world-shaping event, with barely anything real between them, and said
    plainly: we are not being lazy, we are building a legacy. This sets the
    MINIMUM shape every origin's campaign must take before it counts as done:

      Origin intro                -> 15-20 real choice points before the
                                      first common event.
      Between common events        -> 20-30 real choice points.
      Leading into a world event   -> 50+ connected quests/common events
                                      that are THEMATICALLY RELATED to that
                                      specific world event (not generic
                                      filler slotted in to hit a number)
                                      before the world event resolves.
      After a world event          -> keep building the same way toward the
                                      next one, all the way to the last
                                      world event. The campaign does not
                                      thin out as it goes on.

    A "choice point" is a scene with 2+ real options (including a stat check
    split per rule 9). A single-Continue scene that exists purely for
    pacing/breathing room (rule 6) is still valuable and still belongs in
    the mix, but does not count toward this total on its own.

    What this density is FOR, not just a number to hit:
      - build emotion and anticipation before a big swing lands
      - seed lore the player discovers rather than being told
      - give every recurring NPC a real background, not a one-line dossier
      - show culture (how people here actually talk, eat, grieve, celebrate,
        argue) instead of asserting it exists in a summary paragraph
      - let a small early choice teach a lesson the player recognizes,
        unprompted, when a much bigger choice echoes it later

    This is a floor, not permission to pad. Every one of these choice
    points still answers to the SCENE-BY-SCENE WRITING CHECKLIST below, and
    every one still obeys rule 9 - volume without a specific reaction is the
    exact failure rule 9 exists to stop, just spread across more scenes
    instead of concentrated in one. When many choices are close variations
    on the same tactic (tone, internal reflection, minor tradecraft) rather
    than genuinely different actions, it's acceptable to converge them on
    one shared next scene AS LONG AS that scene carries a distinct
    `narrativeVariant` per choice - full new scenes are for outcomes that
    actually diverge (a stat check's success/fail, a different NPC met, a
    different world state produced), not required for every single choice
    regardless of how small the difference is.

10,000-PAGE PRODUCTION STRUCTURE
--------------------------------
Do not try to write 10,000 pages as one linear script. Build a reusable novel
engine across many lives:

  1 life = 8–15 event arcs + personal routes + travel/aftermath scenes
  1 event arc = 12–25 scenes, including 2–4 quiet relationship scenes
  1 Signature NPC = 6–12 encounters spread over several events
  1 long route = 3 acts, each with a reversal and a cost
  1 replay = different origin, faction standing, NPC survival, legacy state,
             and event outcome; never merely different labels on one scene

The page goal is reached through combinable scenes and revisitable people, not
through one bloated mandatory path. Keep a route matrix as content grows:
  origin x event outcome x relationship state x legacy state.

for "50+ connected quests/common events" leading into a world event. The
existing world events (event1-8) are SHARED across all 7 origins - forcing
50+ Wanderer-exclusive pieces into each would make Wanderer's presence in
shared content wildly disproportionate to every other origin's. Until
directed otherwise, treat the origin-specific track above as satisfying
rule 10 for Wanderer, and treat "the 50+ world-event tier" as something
built ONCE per world event (shared, origin-agnostic depth), not duplicated
7 times per origin. Do not start building Wanderer-exclusive content
inside event1-8 without confirming that's actually wanted first - it's a
materially different, and much larger, scope than another intro+events
track.

NPC DOSSIERS — FIRST PLAYABLE ARC
---------------------------------

OLD HENDRIK | Mentor | Civilian-linked
First impression: feeds a too-small fire beside a failing cart; jokes before
asking for anything.
Public want: reach his niece's farm and pass on useful work before his body
will not let him do it.
Private wound: knows he is dying; treats every practical lesson as if it may
be the last one he has time to give.
Voice: patient, metaphorical in practical objects; repeats old stories with
small changes.
Feeling progression:
  stranger -> amused caution -> quiet regard -> paternal honesty -> grief or
  pride, depending on the player's choices.
Route hooks: Mentor, Redemption, Sacrifice. He should never become romance.
Dialogue seed:
  @Hendrik: Most luck is one of two things: a nail, or the hand willing to
  use it. People just prefer the word luck because it asks less of them.
Author rule: Hendrik's advice must be useful but incomplete. He is not the
writer's mouthpiece and can be wrong about what Mira needs.

MIRA TALBOT | Ally | Civilian-linked
First impression: swearing at a fence post instead of admitting fear.
Public want: keep the Talbot farm alive through winter.
Private wound: shame over stolen grain and terror of being seen as the kind of
person hunger can make.
Voice: short sentences; dry jokes only when scared enough to need one.
Feeling progression:
  guarded -> testing -> relief/defiance -> trust or estrangement -> a later
  adult friendship, resentment, or debt that cannot be cleanly repaid.
Route hooks: Redemption, Love (slow-burn only after multiple later arcs),
  Isolation, Revenge against institutions that failed the valley.
Dialogue seed:
  @Mira: I did not steal because I thought I was clever. I stole because the
  sack was there and my little brother was counting crusts.
Author rule: do not make Mira grateful on demand. A gift can relieve her; it
does not automatically erase humiliation, suspicion, or disagreement.

BAILIFF CORIN REYES | Enemy / possible redemption figure
First impression: clean cuffs in a muddy village; knows every statute and
notices every missing sack.
Public want: preserve order before fear turns into a mob.
Private wound: grew up poor and believes ruthless enforcement is the only way
to prevent people like his younger self from being crushed by chaos.
Voice: quotes a statute, then pauses as though listening for an objection he
does not want to hear.
Feeling progression:
  formal contempt -> professional respect -> moral irritation -> fracture ->
  hardening or redemption through visible mercy, never one speech.
Route hooks: Redemption, Justice/Revenge, Diplomacy.
Dialogue seed:
  @Reyes: The law is not kind. It is what remains when kindness has already
  chosen favorites.
Author rule: give Reyes at least one scene where he helps someone without
credit. The player must discover that contradiction before deciding what he is.

GUILDMISTRESS YEVA SOLT | Merchant / ally of convenience
First impression: fixes a torn ledger page before she looks at the speaker.
Public want: protect and expand her guild's place on the Salt Road.
Private wound: fears every kindness will be treated as weakness or debt.
Voice: starts bargains with "And what is in it for you?"; warmth appears as
precision, not sentiment.
Feeling progression:
  appraisal -> transaction -> chosen confidence -> loyalty tested by Petra ->
  partnership or bitter respect.
Route hooks: Love only after partnership and vulnerability; Power, Diplomacy,
  Revenge against the crown's levy system.
Dialogue seed:
  @Yeva: Charity is a word wealthy people use when they want applause for
  paying a debt late.
Author rule: Yeva's orphanage secret earns no instant halo. Let the player see
the cost of her choices before learning the tenderness behind them.

PETRA VOSS | Rival
First impression: makes herself sound less important than she is, then leaves
the room with everyone repeating her idea.
Public want: replace Yeva and prove she can protect the guild better.
Private wound: suspects she is only valued when she is useful, so she turns
every relationship into a contest before it can reject her.
Voice: self-deprecation sharpened into a weapon.
Feeling progression:
  charm -> competition -> ideological conflict -> intimacy or betrayal ->
  grudging alliance, victory, or death by the event rules.
Route hooks: Rivalry, Love, Power, Revenge.
Dialogue seed:
  @Petra: Oh, I would never say Yeva is afraid. I only notice she mistakes
  waiting for wisdom whenever someone else must pay the price.
Author rule: Petra must sometimes be right. A rival who is only cruel is not a
rival; they are a delayed enemy.

TAMSIN THE BELL-RINGER | Wildcard
First impression: knows the news before the people in it know they made it.
Public want: be needed as the person who knows.
Private wound: cannot bear being forgettable; mixes truth and invention until
even she loses track of which is which.
Voice: "You did not hear this from me," followed by something too specific.
Feeling progression:
  amusing rumor -> useful source -> dangerous distortion -> a choice about
  whether truth matters more than the comfort it gives.
Route hooks: Suspense, Revenge, Corruption.
Dialogue seed:
  @Tamsin: You did not hear this from me, which is fortunate, because I have
  already told three people and all of them promised they cannot read.
Author rule: every Tamsin rumor requires one verifiable fact and one uncertain
claim. Do not reveal which is which immediately.

WANDERER NPC DOSSIERS — INTRO CHAIN (wanderer_intro.json)
-----------------------------------------------------------
Written for the rule-10 Wanderer overhaul. All six are the existing
Signature NPCs from `lib/data/npcs.dart` (origin `wanderer`) except Osric,
a backstory figure referenced but never previously named.

OSRIC | Backstory (deceased before the game begins) | Wanderer-linked
First impression: never on-page - known only through the button the player
carries and the lesson they can't stop repeating in his voice.
Public want (in life): to make sure the player survived a road that had
already taken people he loved.
Private wound: never given a page of his own on purpose - he exists to be
carried, not resolved. Do not write a flashback scene that "explains" him
fully; the player's uncertainty about him (loyalty or cowardice, love or
guilt) is the point and should stay live the whole game.
Voice (as remembered, never as dialogue tags): short, unsentimental
instructions dressed as observations.
Route hooks: Isolation (he is the reason solitude feels safe), Redemption
(finally deciding what keeping the button means).
Author rule: Osric never appears as a ghost, vision, or literal flashback
scene with his own @Osric: lines. He is remembered, quoted secondhand,
never staged. If a future author is tempted to give him a full scene,
that's a sign the story has drifted from "the road that made you a
Wanderer" toward a different, more literal kind of story.

DENN HALLOWAY | Rival | Wanderer-linked
First impression: challenges the player to something before introducing
himself - a race, a bet, a dare with real stakes.
Public want: to be remembered as the greatest road-walker the Realm has
ever produced.
Private wound: deeply lonely; rivalry is the only shape of closeness he
knows how to offer or accept without flinching.
Voice: turns everything into a wager, even sincerity ("Bet you didn't
expect me to mean that").
Feeling progression:
  irritation -> reluctant respect -> genuine rivalry -> the choice to keep
  competing forever or let him actually be a friend, which he will not ask
  for directly.
Route hooks: Rivalry resolving into Love or true Friendship (rare,
requires the player to break the competition pattern first), Isolation if
always beaten and never engaged with as a person.
Dialogue seed:
  @Denn: Losing to a stranger's one thing. Losing to a stranger who didn't
  even know we were racing - that one's going to itch for a while.
Author rule: Denn must lose sometimes without becoming bitter, and win
sometimes without gloating past the point of fun. He is competitive, not
cruel - if he starts reading as cruel, the voice has drifted.

OLD FERRICK | Merchant | Wanderer-linked
First impression: perched on a cart loaded past reason, doing visible math
on the value of whoever's approaching.
Public want: enough coin to stop moving and open one real, permanent shop.
Private wound: has been running the same trust-then-vanish con so long he's
half-convinced himself it's a fair trade for what he offers.
Voice: prices everything, including conversation ("that'll cost you a
story, not just coin").
Feeling progression:
  transactional curiosity -> either genuine trade (if the player doesn't
  take the bait) or betrayal (if they trust him with something he can
  carry off) -> a standing reputation that follows him into later scenes.
Route hooks: minor Redemption (rare, if confronted and chooses honesty over
the pattern), otherwise a recurring cautionary/comic figure, not a deep
route on his own.
Dialogue seed:
  @Ferrick: Everything on this cart has a price. Some of it isn't coin.
Author rule: never let Ferrick's theft be the ONLY outcome available to a
player who engages with him - a player who buys properly and doesn't offer
temptation should get a completely honest transaction, every time.

THE TOLLKEEPER | Enemy | Wanderer-linked
First impression: dressed too well for a bandit, speaks too formally to be
anything else, blocking a road that was free yesterday.
Public want: total, unchallenged control of a stretch of the Old Salt Road.
Private wound: a fired Crownlands tax officer (twenty-two years' service,
dismissed over a ledger shortfall that wasn't his doing), replicating
unofficially and worse the exact system that discarded him.
Voice: formal, archaic phrasing even mid-threat; treats extortion like a
transaction owed to him personally. Cracks under real pressure - by his
escalation event, the composure is visibly fraying.
Feeling progression:
  performative politeness (intro, first contact) -> real threat once
  refused -> ESCALATION (wanderer_event_tollkeeper.json): hostages,
  including Old Ferrick, when the toll alone stops working for him ->
  one of four built endings depending on how he's stopped and what the
  player chooses afterward: crown justice (trial, not execution - ties to
  Bailiff Reyes's established "not irredeemable" dossier note), released
  with a warning, offered a genuine path to honest work, or dead.
Route hooks: Revenge (his, against the crown - PARTIALLY explored: his
grievance monologue exists, a full revenge-route payoff doesn't yet),
Power (the player's, if they took over his racket instead of ending it -
still unexplored, still flagged), Mercy as its own tracked honor trigger
(now built - see the disposition choice in his event).
Dialogue seed:
  @Tollkeeper: Passage is ten silver. Refusal is also an option, of course.
  Every option is an option, until it isn't.
Author rule: a Royal Heir player must get a variant that references his
firing specifically - this is one of the few NPCs whose grievance is
directly, personally about House Aldric, not abstract class resentment.

SAOIRSE BELLWETHER | Mentor | Wanderer-linked
First impression: doesn't introduce herself - answers a question the
player hadn't finished asking yet.
Public want: to find out, honestly, whether this Wanderer is different
from the ones who eventually gave up the road.
Private wound (RESOLVED, trust-gated - see below): was bodyguard to a
Royal Heir; let Lady Ysolde Marrow into a private audience she shouldn't
have had clearance for, watched her charge change afterward, started
asking questions, and left before anyone decided what to do about that.
Three years later, Marrow's agents finally find her.
Voice: answers questions with harder questions; teaches by making the
player notice something themselves rather than being told. Cracks, once,
into real vulnerability during her event - deliberate, not a voice
inconsistency (see her event's final dossier note below).
Feeling progression:
  tested -> grudging usefulness proven -> real teaching -> (if
  npc.saoirse_bellwether.trust >= 3) her past resurfaces and the
  relationship either ends (she disappears into a new identity), deepens
  into companionship (she stays on the road), or transforms into
  something bigger (she resolves to confront Marrow directly, seeding
  future Royal Heir content).
Route hooks: Mentor; the Wanderer's SECOND common event
(`wanderer_event_saoirse_past.json`, trust-gated at 3+, else bypassed
entirely) - 15 scenes, 8 real choice points so far, ties directly into
Lady Ysolde Marrow (established Royal Heir antagonist) without
contradicting her existing dossier - this is a genuine cross-origin plot
thread, not a coincidence of shared naming. If `world_flags.saoirse
_resolution == 'confronts_marrow'`, a future Royal Heir/Succession Crisis
author has a live, player-earned hook to bring Saoirse back into that
storyline directly.
Dialogue seed:
  @Saoirse: I didn't ask if you were scared. I asked what you did next.
  Those are different questions and only one of them is useful to me.
Author rule: Saoirse's lessons must be incomplete on purpose (same
constraint as Hendrik) - she is teaching survival, not delivering the
correct answer to the game.

CORWIN ASHE | Ally | Wanderer-linked
First impression: methodical, unhurried, checking a worn map by firelight
like he's done it a thousand times without once finding what he's looking
for.
Public want: find the sister he lost track of three winters ago.
Private wound (RESOLVED - see below): stayed silent while their parents
arranged Elsa's marriage to a man twice her age over a debt. She left the
same season the wedding would have happened and never told him where -
deliberately, changing her name, specifically so he couldn't find her. He
suspects this himself only partway through the search; the player can
press him toward the full truth or let him arrive at it on his own pace.
Voice: never uses a metaphor when a plain sentence will do; flattens his
own feelings into logistics ("she'd have gone north, probably, if she went
anywhere at all").
Feeling progression:
  practical stranger -> traveling companion -> real vulnerability once the
  trail reveals she hid on purpose -> one of several earned resolutions
  (see below) that permanently shapes whether he stays withdrawn, finds
  peace, or is left in messier, more human uncertainty for the rest of
  the run.
Route hooks: the Wanderer's first common event IS his search, and it is
now FULLY BUILT and clears rule 10's density floor - see
`wanderer_event_corwins_sister.json` (40 scenes, 21 real choice points,
including texture beats: a road-sense callback to Saoirse's lesson,
fishing-village tide culture, the net-mender Bessa's own small story,
Corwin's own childhood before the road, a weather obstacle en route to
Millhaven, Millhaven's guild culture, and a doubt beat before the truth
comes out). Three distinct approach paths at the
bakery (confirm with Elsa first / bring Corwin immediately / watch before
deciding) lead to five different named endings
(`world_flags.corwin_sister_resolution`: earned_reunion, privacy_protected,
told_directly, messy_corwin_defended/elsa_defended/neutral, watched_told,
watched_let_go) - genuinely different outcomes, not one ending with cosmetic
variation. Also seeds Sacrifice (the search costs real time/resources) and
Redemption (if the player initially refuses him and later returns - this
specific branch is NOT yet built; the current file assumes the player
commits at `wanderer_intro_15`).
Dialogue seed:
  @Corwin: She'd have gone north, probably, if she went anywhere at all.
  I've been saying "probably" for three winters. I'm aware of how that
  sounds.
Author rule: never let Corwin find his sister alive through convenient
coincidence. If she's found, the scene must have been earned across
several prior choices, not handed over as a reward beat.

ELSA (goes by "Essa") | Corwin's sister, introduced in wanderer_event_corwins_sister.json
First impression: flour to her elbows, running a small Millhaven bakery,
entirely at home - nothing about her reads like someone who's spent three
years hiding.
Public want: to be somewhere no one, including her own brother, gets to
decide things for her anymore.
Private wound: was nearly married off to a man twice her age over a family
debt; Corwin knew and stayed silent. She doesn't hate him for it, which is
almost worse than if she did - she's just done letting his love make her
choices for her.
Voice: direct, a little wry, unsentimental about her own history in a way
that reads as hard-won rather than cold ("people who rename themselves
usually have their reasons").
Author rule: Elsa is never simply relieved to be found. Every ending
available to the player treats her reaction as something she gets to
determine, not something the search's success or failure decides for her -
this is the entire point of her arc and should not be softened in future
content that touches her.

THE PIEBALD FOOL | Wildcard | Wanderer-linked
First impression: appears where the road bends out of sight of everything
else, always mid-sentence, as if the conversation had already started.
Public want: unclear, possibly not a want in any sense the player can
transact with.
Private wound: none written - deliberately kept unknowable. Do not resolve
this NPC's mystery in a future arc without a very strong reason; the
not-knowing is the function.
Voice: rhymes, sometimes mid-word; answers only the question the player
should have asked, not the one they did.
Feeling progression: none in the conventional sense - this NPC does not
build linear trust. Each encounter stands alone, atmospheric, occasionally
prophetic in hindsight once a world event the Fool alluded to actually
happens.
Route hooks: none directly - a texture/foreshadowing device, not a
relationship route.
Dialogue seed:
  @Piebald Fool: Ask the road what it's carried, not where it's going -
  the road never lies about the first one.
Author rule: the Fool must never give the player mechanically actionable
information (no "go here, do this"). Everything said should read as
significant only in retrospect.

CIVILIAN NPC DOSSIERS — INTRO CHAIN (civilian_intro.json)
-----------------------------------------------------------
Written for the rule-10 Civilian overhaul. Unlike Wanderer, the Civilian
origin's existing Signature NPCs (Mira Talbot, Bailiff Reyes, Yeva Solt,
Old Hendrik, Petra Voss, Tamsin) are ALREADY the shared spine's central
cast, met by every origin, not exclusive to Civilian - so this intro does
NOT re-introduce them. Instead it builds what a Civilian player actually
has that other origins don't: a home, a family, and a hometown (Aldenmoor)
to leave behind, giving the later shared-spine encounters (especially
Harvest Failure) more weight for this origin specifically, since a
Civilian player has just lived through their own smaller version of
"a community depending on you" before the game's first common event asks
the same question at village scale.

AVELINE | Mother | Civilian-linked, hometown-only (Aldenmoor)
First impression: finds the player before they finish deciding how to
find her - an instinct, not a coincidence.
Public want: to send her child off prepared, without either of them
noticing how much that costs her.
Private wound: decided long ago that visible grief was a luxury the farm
couldn't afford; holds to that decision through effort, not ease.
Voice: practical warmth, undercuts her own emotion with logistics
("I'd hoped you'd forget to actually go through with it, if I'm honest").
Route hooks: none directly (hometown-only, not expected to recur) - but
her line about never forgetting anything the player set their mind to is
a deliberate plant for a possible callback if Aldenmoor is ever revisited.
Author rule: never let Aveline cry on-page. The restraint is the character.

NESSA | Younger sister | Civilian-linked, hometown-only (Aldenmoor)
First impression: has spent a week pre-loading her anger about this
morning and hasn't used the head start productively.
Public want: for the player to simply not go, stated as an argument that
both of them know is really a question.
Private wound: nine years younger, too young to have a real plan for what
her home looks like once her sibling's gone.
Voice: deflects real feeling into logic-toned arguments ("Staying is
allowed, you know").
Route hooks: none directly - a possible reunion/return beat if Aldenmoor
is ever revisited later in the campaign (unbuilt).
Author rule: Nessa's scene must end on the player's terms (gift, promise,
or humor) - never resolve her feelings FOR her; she's still upset when the
scene ends, however the player handled it, and that's correct.

ELDER MAREN | Village elder | Civilian-linked, hometown-only (Aldenmoor)
First impression: already waiting at the crossroads shrine, having timed
this goodbye the way she's timed others before it.
Public want: to send every departing child off with something true to
carry, not just ceremony.
Private wound: unwritten on purpose - she is function-first here (a
blessing-giver), not a deep route target.
Voice: plain, slightly weathered wisdom, deliberately undercuts her own
authority ("doesn't matter out there... only cares what you do with what
