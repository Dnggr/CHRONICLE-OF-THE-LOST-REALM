================================================================================
CHRONICLE OF THE LOST REALM — NPC DIALOGUE, FEELINGS & ROUTES
NOVEL-PACING AUTHORING FILE
================================================================================

PURPOSE
-------
This is the prose companion to story_systems_bible_EXPANDED.txt. The Bible
defines systems, flags, and event outcomes. This file defines how named people
feel, speak, conceal things, and change across long-form play.

The target is novel pacing: context before crisis; a person before a quest;
aftermath after consequence. A scene must not exist solely to deliver a choice.
It should leave the player with an image, an unanswered question, or a changed
feeling toward someone.

NON-NEGOTIABLE PACING RULES
---------------------------
1. Introduce a place before asking the player to save it. Use weather, work,
   smell, local custom, overheard talk, and one small ordinary problem.
2. Introduce an NPC in at least two beats: first an observable action, then a
   conversation. Do not open with their secret, biography, or quest request.
3. Between major event stages, write 2–4 breathing scenes: travel, a campfire,
   a letter, a meal, a rumor, an argument, or a quiet aftermath.
4. Every important NPC needs one mundane want and one private wound. The
   mundane want makes them believable; the wound creates story pressure.
5. Violence is an escalation, never filler. Gore must change the survivor,
   relationship, reputation, or world state. Do not use it only for shock.
6. Love, revenge, redemption, suspense, and drama are slow-burn patterns.
   No route may resolve on a first meeting or one perfect dialogue choice.
7. A choice should cost time, trust, safety, money, information, or a future
   opportunity. The player should feel what they gave up, not only what gained.
8. Never force a player to read a lore dump to proceed. Curiosity opens lore;
   urgency advances the plot; both remain valid play styles.

10,000-PAGE PRODUCTION STRUCTURE
--------------------------------
Do not try to write 10,000 pages as one linear script. Build a reusable novel
engine across many lives:

  1 life = 8–15 event arcs + personal routes + travel/aftermath scenes
  1 event arc = 12–25 scenes, including 2–4 quiet relationship scenes
  1 Signature NPC = 6–12 encounters spread over several events
  1 long route = 3 acts, each with a reversal and a cost
  1 replay = different origin, faction standing, NPC survival, legacy state,
             and event outcome; never merely different labels on one scene

The page goal is reached through combinable scenes and revisitable people, not
through one bloated mandatory path. Keep a route matrix as content grows:
  origin x event outcome x relationship state x legacy state.

CURRENT PLAYABLE SEQUENCE
-------------------------
Origin opening -> Old Hendrik roadside scene -> caravan/ruin -> Harvest
Failure -> Mira Talbot -> Salt Road Rebellion -> Event 2 warning.

Old Hendrik is intentionally introduced before Mira. He gives the player a
human reason to care about the Talbot farm before the game asks for help. His
warning also frames Harvest Failure as a social crisis, not merely a flag.

NPC DOSSIERS — FIRST PLAYABLE ARC
---------------------------------

OLD HENDRIK | Mentor | Civilian-linked
First impression: feeds a too-small fire beside a failing cart; jokes before
asking for anything.
Public want: reach his niece's farm and pass on useful work before his body
will not let him do it.
Private wound: knows he is dying; treats every practical lesson as if it may
be the last one he has time to give.
Voice: patient, metaphorical in practical objects; repeats old stories with
small changes.
Feeling progression:
  stranger -> amused caution -> quiet regard -> paternal honesty -> grief or
  pride, depending on the player's choices.
Route hooks: Mentor, Redemption, Sacrifice. He should never become romance.
Dialogue seed:
  @Hendrik: Most luck is one of two things: a nail, or the hand willing to
  use it. People just prefer the word luck because it asks less of them.
Author rule: Hendrik's advice must be useful but incomplete. He is not the
writer's mouthpiece and can be wrong about what Mira needs.

MIRA TALBOT | Ally | Civilian-linked
First impression: swearing at a fence post instead of admitting fear.
Public want: keep the Talbot farm alive through winter.
Private wound: shame over stolen grain and terror of being seen as the kind of
person hunger can make.
Voice: short sentences; dry jokes only when scared enough to need one.
Feeling progression:
  guarded -> testing -> relief/defiance -> trust or estrangement -> a later
  adult friendship, resentment, or debt that cannot be cleanly repaid.
Route hooks: Redemption, Love (slow-burn only after multiple later arcs),
  Isolation, Revenge against institutions that failed the valley.
Dialogue seed:
  @Mira: I did not steal because I thought I was clever. I stole because the
  sack was there and my little brother was counting crusts.
Author rule: do not make Mira grateful on demand. A gift can relieve her; it
does not automatically erase humiliation, suspicion, or disagreement.

BAILIFF CORIN REYES | Enemy / possible redemption figure
First impression: clean cuffs in a muddy village; knows every statute and
notices every missing sack.
Public want: preserve order before fear turns into a mob.
Private wound: grew up poor and believes ruthless enforcement is the only way
to prevent people like his younger self from being crushed by chaos.
Voice: quotes a statute, then pauses as though listening for an objection he
does not want to hear.
Feeling progression:
  formal contempt -> professional respect -> moral irritation -> fracture ->
  hardening or redemption through visible mercy, never one speech.
Route hooks: Redemption, Justice/Revenge, Diplomacy.
Dialogue seed:
  @Reyes: The law is not kind. It is what remains when kindness has already
  chosen favorites.
Author rule: give Reyes at least one scene where he helps someone without
credit. The player must discover that contradiction before deciding what he is.

GUILDMISTRESS YEVA SOLT | Merchant / ally of convenience
First impression: fixes a torn ledger page before she looks at the speaker.
Public want: protect and expand her guild's place on the Salt Road.
Private wound: fears every kindness will be treated as weakness or debt.
Voice: starts bargains with "And what is in it for you?"; warmth appears as
precision, not sentiment.
Feeling progression:
  appraisal -> transaction -> chosen confidence -> loyalty tested by Petra ->
  partnership or bitter respect.
Route hooks: Love only after partnership and vulnerability; Power, Diplomacy,
  Revenge against the crown's levy system.
Dialogue seed:
  @Yeva: Charity is a word wealthy people use when they want applause for
  paying a debt late.
Author rule: Yeva's orphanage secret earns no instant halo. Let the player see
the cost of her choices before learning the tenderness behind them.

PETRA VOSS | Rival
First impression: makes herself sound less important than she is, then leaves
the room with everyone repeating her idea.
Public want: replace Yeva and prove she can protect the guild better.
Private wound: suspects she is only valued when she is useful, so she turns
every relationship into a contest before it can reject her.
Voice: self-deprecation sharpened into a weapon.
Feeling progression:
  charm -> competition -> ideological conflict -> intimacy or betrayal ->
  grudging alliance, victory, or death by the event rules.
Route hooks: Rivalry, Love, Power, Revenge.
Dialogue seed:
  @Petra: Oh, I would never say Yeva is afraid. I only notice she mistakes
  waiting for wisdom whenever someone else must pay the price.
Author rule: Petra must sometimes be right. A rival who is only cruel is not a
rival; they are a delayed enemy.

TAMSIN THE BELL-RINGER | Wildcard
First impression: knows the news before the people in it know they made it.
Public want: be needed as the person who knows.
Private wound: cannot bear being forgettable; mixes truth and invention until
even she loses track of which is which.
Voice: "You did not hear this from me," followed by something too specific.
Feeling progression:
  amusing rumor -> useful source -> dangerous distortion -> a choice about
  whether truth matters more than the comfort it gives.
Route hooks: Suspense, Revenge, Corruption.
Dialogue seed:
  @Tamsin: You did not hear this from me, which is fortunate, because I have
  already told three people and all of them promised they cannot read.
Author rule: every Tamsin rumor requires one verifiable fact and one uncertain
claim. Do not reveal which is which immediately.

SCENE-BY-SCENE WRITING CHECKLIST
--------------------------------
Before adding scene JSON, answer:
- Whose point of view is emotionally centered here?
- What does the player notice before anyone explains it?
- What does each present NPC want in this exact minute?
- What feeling should remain after the choice?
- What later scene can call back to one image, line, or promise here?
- Which route is being slowly nourished, threatened, or complicated?

Use `npc.<id>.trust`, `npc.<id>.status`, reputation tracks, and world flags
only after the prose makes the change emotionally legible. Mechanics record
the consequence; prose earns it.
