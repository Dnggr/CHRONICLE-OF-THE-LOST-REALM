0. HOW TO READ THIS DOCUMENT (read this first)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
This is a design document, not code — it's meant to be the reference you
(and any AI co-writer, including future-Claude) work FROM when authoring
actual scene JSON. It uses the engine vocabulary already built into the
project (originTags, world_flags, ConditionEvaluator, SceneChoice.effects,
DeceasedHero, etc.) so every system here is something you can actually
wire up with what exists today, not a wishlist that needs a new engine.

EXPANSION NOTE: section 3 (World Events System) has been rebuilt into a
full CAMPAIGN SPINE — every catalog event now has a lead-in (PRE-ARC), an
optional lore-focused minor route, an optional lesson-focused route, its
original stages expanded with explicit branch options, concrete NPC-death
conditions, new NPCs the event itself produces, and a lead-out (POST-ARC)
that bridges into the next event on the spine, in order, from the
already-built Harvest Failure through the Demon Lord capstone. What comes
after the very last event is handled by the formal Living Legend / Retired
Hero system (3.14): a surviving hero leaves the playable run but can
persist as a findable person in later runs. The NEXT playthrough is still
the real answer to "what happens after," not a fixed epilogue. Everything
else in the document is unchanged from the prior edition.

SCOPE NOTE on "500 unique NPCs" — read this before you judge the NPC
section light on named characters:
Hand-authoring 500 fully fleshed, individually-named characters in one
document would not actually give you 500 characters who "feel like real
people" — past the first 40-ish, it becomes name-and-adjective padding,
which is worse than not having them. Instead, this document gives you:
  (a) 42 SIGNATURE NPCs — fully authored, named, with personality,
      voice, secrets, and story arcs — 6 per origin (Ally, Enemy,
      Merchant, Mentor, Rival, Wildcard) x 7 origins. These are your
      main cast: the characters who actually recur across the branching
      story and interact with the Legacy Engine.
  (b) A GENERATION FRAMEWORK (section 2.4) — trait/motivation/flaw/voice
      tables you combine to author BACKGROUND NPCs on demand, as scenes
      need them. The tables alone produce well over 500 non-repeating
      combinations, and because every table entry is a concrete,
      specific detail (not a generic placeholder), a background NPC
      built from three table rolls still reads as a specific person, not
      a randomized stat block.
This is how a solo dev (with or without an AI co-writer) actually reaches
"the world feels full of real people" without 500 hand-written character
sheets nobody will ever finish, let alone maintain.

1. WORLD PRIMER (context for everything below)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
Setting recap (from the existing prologue + Royal Heir archetype):
- Ruling house: House Aldric, currently King Aldric III (WorldHistory
  default monarchInPower).
- The Sun Temple: a holy site, destroyed in a prior playthrough if
  world_flags.sun_temple_destroyed is true — already wired into the
  engine as your first example of a world-shaping past choice.
- The realm is unnamed so far — for this document it's referred to as
  "the Realm" or "Aldric's Realm." Name it properly when you're ready;
  nothing here depends on the name.

Three broad regions to hang content on (expand freely):
- The Capital & Crownlands — House Aldric's seat, court intrigue,
  Royal Heir content lives here.
- The Hollow Reaches — wilderness, ruins, the Sun Temple, Elf/Dwarf/
  Wanderer content, most of the "danger and old magic" content.
- The Salt Road — trade routes and border towns, Civilian/Mercenary/
  Vampire content, most of the "people and politics" content.

2. NPC SYSTEM
--------------------------------------------------------------------------

2.1 NPC ROLES (expanded beyond Ally/Enemy/Merchant)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
Every origin gets at least one NPC in EACH of these six roles. More roles
than the user's original three (Ally/Enemy/Merchant) because a world
that only has "friend, foe, shopkeeper" reads thin fast:

- ALLY — helps the player advance the main plot; can die, betray you, or
  be betrayed BY you (see Honor System, section 4).
- ENEMY — actively works against the player; not always evil — some
  enemies are enemies because of what YOU did in a past playthrough
  (see Legacy Engine hooks below).
- MERCHANT — sells goods/services; has their own agenda beyond
  commerce (debt, a missing relative, a smuggling operation).
- MENTOR — teaches the player a skill or worldview; mentors in this
  structure are allowed to be WRONG about things, and the player can
  outgrow or reject them (feeds the Redemption/Isolation routes).
- RIVAL — wants the same THING the player wants (a title, a relic, a
  person's affection) but isn't a straightforward enemy — rivalry can
  resolve into respect, romance, or genuine hatred depending on choices.
- WILDCARD — doesn't fit a clean role; a chaotic element who might help
  or hurt depending on player choices, mood, or world state. Wildcards
  are where most of the game's dark humor and unpredictability lives.

2.2 NPC DATA MODEL (for when this becomes engine content)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
When you're ready to formalize this in Dart/JSON, each NPC should carry:

  npc_id            unique slug, e.g. "npc_thessaly_vane"
  display_name      "Thessaly Vane"
  role              ally | enemy | merchant | mentor | rival | wildcard
  linked_origin     which Archetype they're the "corresponding NPC" for
                     (nullable — background NPCs aren't origin-locked)
  core_trait        one-line personality anchor (see table 2.4.A)
  motivation        what they actually want (see table 2.4.B)
  flaw_or_secret    what could break them or what they're hiding (2.4.C)
  voice_pattern     a speech quirk so their @Name: dialogue lines are
                     recognizable without a dialogue tag (2.4.D)
  relationship      current standing with the player, tracked per-NPC
                     (see Honor System, section 4.2) — NOT a single
                     global number
  status_flags      alive / dead / estranged / turned_enemy / etc,
                     stored the same way DeceasedHero tracks fallen
                     PLAYER characters — NPCs deserve the same treatment
  first_scene       where the player can first meet them
  death_conditions  what player choices/world events can kill them
  legacy_hook       how THIS NPC reacts to WorldHistory.deceasedHeroes —
                     e.g. a NPC who was allied with a PAST player
                     character treats the new player differently

Mechanically: extend CharacterState (or a new NpcRelationship model) with
a Map<String, NpcRelationship> keyed by npc_id, so ConditionEvaluator can
gain a new pattern: "npc.<npc_id>.trust >= N" or
"npc.<npc_id>.status == 'dead'" — same syntax family as the existing
flags.<key> / origin.<tag> patterns, so it's a small, consistent addition
rather than a new subsystem.

WILDCARD — The Piebald Fool | Core trait: unreadable, possibly touched

2.4 GENERATION FRAMEWORK — building the other 458+ background NPCs
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
Roll or hand-pick one item from each table below to build a background
NPC in under two minutes, ready to drop into any scene. The tables below
are intentionally SPECIFIC (not "brave" or "shy") so combinations don't
read as generic.

TABLE 2.4.A — CORE TRAIT (30 entries; sample of 15 shown, extend freely)
1. Counts everything out loud without noticing
2. Never sits with their back to a door
3. Corrects people's grammar mid-crisis
4. Names every animal they own, including livestock they'll eat
5. Can't lie convincingly and has stopped trying
6. Remembers insults from a decade ago, forgets kindnesses from
   yesterday
7. Apologizes before disagreeing, every single time
8. Only trusts people who've seen them fail at something
9. Collects a specific useless object (buttons, bottlecaps, bad poetry)
10. Speaks to animals like they'll answer back
11. Finishes other people's sentences, usually wrong
12. Has an opinion about bread specifically, strongly
13. Cannot walk past a debt unpaid, theirs or anyone else's
14. Treats every goodbye like it might be permanent
15. Laughs at the wrong moments, always has

TABLE 2.4.B — MOTIVATION (30 entries; sample of 15 shown)
1. Paying off a debt they didn't personally incur
2. Proving a dead parent wrong about them
3. Protecting a secret that isn't even theirs to keep
4. Earning back a title/position they were stripped of
5. Finding someone who vanished without explanation
6. Undoing one specific mistake, however long it takes
7. Being the first in their family to survive past a certain age
8. Keeping a promise made to someone who's since died
9. Avoiding becoming exactly like a parent/mentor
10. Building something that will outlast them
11. Being believed, after a long time of not being believed
12. Getting far enough away that the past can't follow
13. Earning a place in a community that hasn't fully accepted them
14. Making amends they're not sure are even wanted anymore
15. Simply surviving the season — no bigger plan than that

TABLE 2.4.C — FLAW OR SECRET (30 entries; sample of 15 shown)
1. Owes money to someone dangerous
2. Was complicit in something they've never admitted to
3. Is estranged from family over something minor that calcified
4. Has a habit that will eventually get them killed
5. Lied about their past to everyone they currently know
6. Is in love with someone who doesn't know it
7. Made a promise they now realize they can't keep
8. Is slowly losing a skill/sense central to their identity
9. Betrayed someone once and got away with it clean
10. Believes something about themselves that isn't true
11. Is hiding an injury/illness from people who'd worry
12. Has taken credit for someone else's work, once, and never corrected
    it
13. Is more afraid of a specific small thing than anything "reasonable"
14. Genuinely doesn't know they're the villain of someone else's story
15. Is waiting for a good moment to leave that will never come

TABLE 2.4.D — VOICE/SPEECH PATTERN (20 entries; sample of 12 shown)
1. Trails off mid-sentence when nervous
2. Uses trade/craft jargon for everything, even feelings
3. Answers questions with the opposite question
4. Overexplains simple things, underexplains complicated ones
5. Speaks in short declaratives, avoids qualifiers entirely
6. Quotes a specific book/scripture constantly, often wrong
7. Softens bad news with unrelated compliments first
8. Talks to themselves mid-conversation, audibly
9. Never uses contractions, formal to a fault
10. Curses creatively, never repeats the same one twice
11. Repeats the last few words of what you just said, thinking
12. Goes quiet entirely when it matters most

TABLE 2.4.E — RELATIONSHIP-TO-PLAYER TEMPLATES (15 entries; sample of 8)
1. Owes the player from an earlier scene, resents the debt
2. Mistakes the player for someone from their past
3. Sizes the player up against a Deceased Hero from a prior playthrough
4. Wants something FROM the player before offering any help
5. Is testing the player deliberately, for reasons of their own
6. Already has an opinion of the player based on reputation (see Honor
   System, section 4) before ever meeting them
7. Needs the player's specific origin/skills for one narrow purpose
8. Doesn't care who the player is at all — purely transactional

MATH CHECK: 30 traits x 30 motivations x 30 flaws x 20 voices x 15
relationship templates = well over 8 million unique combinations before
even touching names or locations. You will run out of story before you
run out of NPC combinations — the constraint was never volume, it's
picking combinations that serve the scene you're writing.

3. WORLD EVENTS SYSTEM & THE CAMPAIGN SPINE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
REVISION NOTE (read this first): this section was expanded from the
original World Events System to give every catalog event a full arc —
a lead-in (PRE-ARC), optional lore/lesson side-content, the event itself,
concrete NPC-death conditions, NPCs the event itself produces, and a
lead-out (POST-ARC) that hands off into the next event on the spine. The
goal is a usable backbone for a solo dev (or an AI co-writer) to write
actual scene JSON against, chapter by chapter, without having to invent
the connective tissue between events from scratch each time. Nothing
here removes or contradicts the original catalog — every original
Trigger/Stages/Player intervention/NPC impact/Legacy tie-in block is
preserved inside its event's new EVENT STAGES subsection, expanded
rather than replaced.

3.1 HOW WORLD EVENTS WORK MECHANICALLY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
A World Event is a multi-stage world_flags sequence, NOT a single flag.
Example shape (extending the pattern the Sun Temple flag already uses):

  world_flags.war_with_northern_reach = "brewing" | "active" | "over"
  world_flags.war_outcome = "aldric_victory" | "northern_victory" |
                             "stalemate_treaty" | null

Each stage change is triggered by either (a) enough in-story time passing
(world.currentYear, which already increments on every death — see
LegacyEngine.recordDeath) or (b) a specific major player choice, exactly
like sun_temple_destroyed already works. Scenes check the CURRENT stage
via the existing flags.<key> == 'value' condition syntax — no new syntax
needed, just multi-value flags instead of only booleans (ConditionEvaluator
already supports flags.<key> == 'someString').

Crucially: World Events affect the SIGNATURE NPCs above, not just the
player. An NPC's status_flags can change because of a world event even
if the player never personally interacts with that event — e.g. Captain
Yusra Wielding (Mercenary enemy) might die in the war_with_northern_reach
event whether or not the player ever fought her directly, which the
player then learns about secondhand. This is what makes the world feel
alive rather than player-centric.

Two additions the expanded catalog below leans on constantly, both
additive to the existing model — no engine rewrite required:

  PRE-ARC / POST-ARC — every event below is bracketed by a short run of
  scenes BEFORE its first stage (foreshadowing, a chance to notice
  warning signs, a chance to prepare or ignore them) and a short run of
  scenes AFTER its final stage (the world absorbing the outcome). These
  aren't new stages in the world_flags sense — they're ordinary scenes
  gated on the stage BEFORE the event starts (e.g. flags.<key> == null
  or a dedicated "_rumors" pre-flag) and the stage AFTER it resolves
  (flags.<key> == 'over' or a specific outcome value). Mechanically
  identical to writing any other scene; the only discipline required is
  writing them at all instead of jumping straight from "nothing" to
  "stage 1."

  cascadeTriggers — already specified in 7.2 below: an optional
  {finalOutcome: nextEventId} map checked when an event resolves. The
  campaign spine in 3.2 is the DEFAULT path that map implies; 7.2's
  Dark Legion / Demon Lord entries are folded into the spine as its
  final two events instead of living as a separate "expansion" bolted
  onto the end, since they ARE the end.

3.2 THE CAMPAIGN SPINE — DEFAULT CRITICAL-PATH ORDER
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
The catalog in the original document treated events as mostly
independent, which is correct for how the ENGINE checks them (any flag
can technically fire whenever its trigger condition is met) but not
correct for how a WRITER should approach authoring them. You need a
default order to write toward, even if the branching state machine can
in practice deviate from it. This is that order:

  EVENT 0  — The Harvest Failure           (already built, Chapter 1)
  EVENT 1  — The Rebellion of the Salt Road  (early game)
  EVENT 2  — The War with the Northern Reach (early-mid game)
  EVENT 3  — The Sun Temple Schism           (mid game, CONDITIONAL)
  EVENT 4  — The Plague out of the Hollow Reaches (mid game)
  EVENT 5  — The Succession Crisis           (mid-late game)
  EVENT 6  — Foreign Invasion from Across the Salt Sea (late game)
  EVENT 7  — The Dark Legion                 (cascade, late game)
  EVENT 8  — The Demon Lord                  (capstone, endgame)
  POST-FINALE — Living Legends / Retired Heroes (3.14)

Why this order, specifically:
- Harvest Failure first because it's already built, small-scale, and
  teaches the Honor System's "who do you help when you can't help
  everyone" pattern before anything bigger asks the same question at
  higher stakes.
- Rebellion before War because Rebellion is bottom-up (guilds, common
  folk, a Bailiff's redemption arc) and War is top-down (the crown, a
  foreign power) — starting local before national matches how the
  Origin archetypes are introduced (Civilian/Wanderer content is more
  front-loaded than Royal Heir content in the existing prologue).
- War before Schism/Plague because War is the spine's first EXISTENTIAL
  stake (a named Signature NPC, Ser Aldous Fenn, can die in it) and
  needs to land before quieter, weirder events (Schism, Plague) ask the
  player to sit with grief and scarcity rather than combat.
- Schism is marked CONDITIONAL and slots in wherever
  world_flags.sun_temple_destroyed became true — if it never did, this
  event simply never fires and the spine quietly runs Event 2 -> 4
  instead. Nothing downstream depends on Schism firing; it's a bonus
  branch, not a gate.
- Succession Crisis after Plague because a realm already weakened by
  disease makes a succession fight higher-stakes than it would be
  fresh — and because by this point the player has enough reputation
  history for background NPCs to start recognizing them (see the
  worked example in section 6).
- Foreign Invasion, Dark Legion, and the Demon Lord are kept in strict
  sequence because each is LITERALLY built from the wreckage of what
  came before it (7.2's cascade logic) — Invasion's fractured-realm
  severity scaling feeds Dark Legion's trigger odds, which feeds
  whether the Demon Lord appears at all. This is the one stretch of the
  spine that should NOT be treated as freely reorderable.

Branch logic note: nothing above is a hard gate that blocks the player
from reaching later content if they "did it wrong." Every event has an
outcome range (see each EVENT STAGES block) rather than a pass/fail —
the spine describes which event the world is LIKELY to surface next
given how the previous one resolved, using the same cascadeTriggers
pattern already specified in 7.2, not a locked sequence.

3.3 THE EVENT ARC TEMPLATE — THE SEVEN-PART SHAPE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
Every event from 3.4 onward (except the already-built Harvest Failure,
which gets a lighter treatment) follows this same seven-part shape, so
it's specified once here instead of re-explained nine times:

  PRE-ARC — 2-4 scenes/branch points that exist BEFORE the event's first
  stage. Always includes at least one moment where an attentive player
  (or a specific NPC) could notice the event coming and either brace for
  it or ignore it — the ignoring is a legitimate, unpunished choice, not
  a trap; it just changes which options are available once the event
  starts.

  LORE ROUTE — one OPTIONAL, skippable minor route that exists purely to
  deepen the player's understanding of the world/event's backstory. Never
  required to progress. Usually gated behind curiosity (an extra dialogue
  branch, an out-of-the-way location) rather than a stat check, so it
  rewards attention rather than build optimization.

  LESSON ROUTE — one branch built specifically to teach the player a
  THEMATIC lesson through consequence rather than exposition — the
  player does something, the world responds, and the response IS the
  lesson (no NPC delivers it as a speech). Tied to a concrete mechanical
  hook (a reputation delta, an npc.trust change, a locked/unlocked scene)
  so the lesson has a felt cost or reward, not just narration.

  EVENT STAGES — the original catalog's Trigger/Stages/Player
  intervention/NPC impact/Legacy tie-in block, preserved and expanded
  with explicit per-stage branch options.

  NPC DEATHS — which NPCs (Signature, Notable, or newly introduced by
  this event) CAN die here, under what specific condition, and why the
  death serves the plot rather than existing for shock value. Every
  death listed is avoidable through specific player action unless
  marked "FIXED" (meaning it happens regardless of player choice, for
  pacing/stakes reasons the entry explains).

  NEW NPCS — named characters this event itself produces (refugees,
  officers, opportunists, survivors) rather than pre-existing cast,
  using the same Format per NPC as section 2.3 (Role | Name | Core trait
  | Wants | Voice | Secret | Hook) so they drop into the data model in
  2.2 without translation.

  POST-ARC — 2-4 scenes AFTER the event's final stage, showing the world
  absorbing the outcome, closing loose NPC threads the event opened, and
  explicitly setting up the conditions (per 3.2's cascade logic) for the
  NEXT event on the spine.

3.3.A NOVEL PACING RULE (added 2026-08-02)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
The game is written as a long-form fantasy novel, not a chain of urgent
quest prompts. Before a major choice, establish place, ordinary life,
and at least one person the player can feel about. Before an NPC asks
for help, show them doing something mundane, then let the player talk
with them. After a major choice, give the player aftermath: travel,
silence, rumor, a letter, a meal, grief, or a changed relationship.

Each world event should target 12-25 scenes, including 2-4 quiet
relationship/travel scenes, rather than rushing directly from trigger
to resolution. Love, revenge, redemption, suspense, drama, and gore
must be slow-burn consequences of accumulated choices; violence is not
filler and must change a survivor, relationship, or world state.

The detailed writing reference is `npc_dialogue_and_routes.txt`. It
defines current NPC voice/feeling sheets, route pacing, dialogue seeds,
and the production model for the long-term 10,000-page ambition.

3.14 POST-FINALE — LIVING LEGENDS / RETIRED HEROES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
THIS IS COMMITTED SYSTEM DESIGN, not an optional epilogue idea.

PURPOSE:
A player who survives the final reachable world event may retire their
hero. Retirement ends that hero's playable run exactly as death does,
but does not turn them into a DeceasedHero. Instead, the Legacy Engine
stores a RetiredHero record in permanent WorldHistory. A later character
can find, speak with, help, oppose, recruit, disappoint, or be judged by
that former protagonist as a real NPC. This makes a successful run part
of the next story without letting a player control the same hero forever.

CORE RULES (non-negotiable):
1. Death and retirement are different canon states. A dead hero uses
   recordDeath() and becomes a DeceasedHero. A survivor who chooses
   "Retire and enter the Chronicle" becomes a RetiredHero. Never create
   both records for the same run.
2. Retirement is available only after the run's final reachable event
   has resolved. It cannot undo a fatal choice, NPC death, or event
   outcome.
3. Retirement preserves the past; it does not preserve player control.
   In a new run, the new character is the only playable hero and only
   that character's choices decide the live story.
4. A RetiredHero is a person, not a second protagonist or a permanent
   party member. They can offer a scene-specific boon, information,
   shelter, political access, or limited intervention, but never solve a
   major event off-screen or replace the current hero in a decisive choice.
5. WorldHistory is canonical once written. A new hero cannot rewrite the
   retired hero's choices, but can continue their work, repair harm they
   caused, reject their advice, or make a different future from it.

END-OF-RUN FLOW:
  final event resolves
      -> hero dies: DeceasedHero + death Chronicle entry
      -> hero survives: short closing scene showing consequences
             -> "Retire and enter the Chronicle": RetiredHero +
                retirement Chronicle entry; clear the run
             -> no "keep playing this hero" option in v1

The closing scene assigns an earned retirement role and location, e.g.
"Warden of the Hollow Reaches," "Councillor to the Crown," "Roadside
Healer," "Exile by Choice," or "Unquiet Veteran." This is an encounter
hook, not a promise that every survivor is powerful or celebrated. An
infamous survivor may retire in hiding; an honorable one may be poor,
wounded, or hard to reach.

RETIRED HERO DATA MODEL:
Add `List<RetiredHero> retiredHeroes` to WorldHistory alongside
`deceasedHeroes`. A RetiredHero is a permanent serialized snapshot.
Minimum fields:

  heroId                 stable id; never reused
  name, title            identity shown in scenes and History
  retiredYear, age       chronological placement
  alignment              derived from final reputation, as on death
  retirementRole         the earned posture named above
  location               plausible encounter location
  availability           available | hidden | imprisoned | missing |
                         estranged | dead_after_retirement
  majorCanonEvents       completed events and final outcomes
  relicsLeft             named items retained, hidden, donated, or passed on
  finalReputation        honor, infamy, crown, commonfolk snapshot
  relationshipSeeds      durable prior-run faction/NPC ties only
  legacyStance           mercy | pragmatism | ambition | vengeance;
                         a writing guide, not an AI simulation
  unresolvedThreads      explicit hooks a later hero may pursue
  encounterHistory       per-new-run flags preventing repeated rewards

Do not serialize the whole CharacterState as a live actor. The curated
snapshot keeps saves stable and prevents invisible off-screen progress.

ENCOUNTERS IN A NEW RUN:
At character creation, select at most ONE featured legacy hero: normally
the most recent eligible RetiredHero. Older heroes remain rumours,
History entries, or rare event hooks. The featured hero gets one
guaranteed introductory encounter after the prologue if reachable; later
meetings are gated by the current run's choices and availability. Do not
turn the opening into a parade of former player characters.

Each encounter uses this structure:
  legacy prerequisite -> discovery/rumour -> conversation -> current
  hero choice -> bounded legacy consequence -> updated run/world flag.

Bounded consequences include:
- Help them: gain a clue, one-scene ally, safe route, or a relic they
  voluntarily entrust; pay a cost or accept their faction tie.
- Oppose them: expose their past, deny their request, protect people
  harmed by their policy, or compete for a current objective.
- Continue their work: unlock an unresolved thread, whose resolution
  belongs to the current hero and may differ from what the legend wanted.
- Refuse them: they remain history; no forced punishment, but a later
  opportunity may close.

AGENCY AND PROGRESSION CLARIFICATION:
A retired hero does NOT continue progressing with old inventory, stats,
quests, or a decision tree while the new character is played. Their
snapshot is frozen at retirement. If they appear in an event, their
authored response is selected from `legacyStance`, `finalReputation`,
`majorCanonEvents`, and the CURRENT hero's choice. For example, an
honorable former commander offers soldiers only if asked and the new hero
has not betrayed their faction; an infamous exile withholds a relic unless
the new hero accepts a morally costly bargain. The current player always
chooses whether to seek, accept, resist, or walk away from that influence.

CHANGE OVER TIME AND FINAL FATE:
Retired heroes change only on-screen through an explicit later-run scene.
A result may set availability to missing, imprisoned, or
dead_after_retirement, move them, consume a relic, or resolve a thread.
Write the current hero who caused that change into a Chronicle addendum.
Never roll unseen events that kill or radically transform them between
runs. If one dies in a later run, preserve their RetiredHero record and
mark its final fate; do not duplicate them in `deceasedHeroes`.

IMPLEMENTATION BOUNDARY:
This requires an engine extension. Add `recordRetirement(...)` beside
`LegacyEngine.recordDeath()` to create the snapshot, write the retirement
Chronicle entry, save WorldHistory, and clear the run. Extend History to
separate "Fallen Heroes" and "Living Legends." Scene conditions should
read compact fields only, e.g.
`legacy.retired.<heroId>.availability == 'available'` and
`world_flags.retired_hero.<heroId>.<key>`. Every encounter must state its
one-time reward and state change.

This preserves the legacy loop: death makes a remembered absence;
survival makes a living consequence. Neither allows a new character to
rewrite the past, and both let the current player decide what it means now.

4. THE HONOR / REPUTATION SYSTEM
--------------------------------------------------------------------------

4.1 WHY MULTIPLE TRACKS, NOT ONE "HONOR METER"
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
A single global honor number flattens everything into "good vs evil,"
which doesn't fit a world with factions, rivalries, and origin-specific
politics. Instead, track FOUR separate numbers plus PER-NPC relationship
values:

  reputation.honor       — general "do you keep your word / show mercy"
                            score. Public-facing, affects how STRANGERS
                            react to the player on first meeting.
  reputation.infamy      — separate from honor's inverse — tracks how
                            FEARED/notorious the player is, which can be
                            high even alongside high honor (a feared
                            hero, not just a feared villain).
  reputation.crown       — standing specifically with House Aldric/the
                            law.
  reputation.commonfolk  — standing specifically with ordinary people/
                            guilds — can openly conflict with
                            reputation.crown (Robin-Hood-style plays are
                            possible: high commonfolk, low crown).
  npc.<id>.trust         — the per-NPC relationship value mentioned in
                            section 2.2 — personal, not public.

This lets a player be, simultaneously: high commonfolk reputation, low
crown reputation, high infamy, high honor, AND personally distrusted by
one specific NPC they betrayed — a much richer state space than one
number ever could represent, and it maps directly onto the varied NPC
reactions described throughout section 2.3.

4.2 CONSEQUENCE TRIGGERS (the broad list the user asked for)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
Each trigger below should be implemented as SceneChoice.effects entries
(the mechanism already exists — see the royal/vampire origin-gated
choices in prologue.json for the pattern) writing to the reputation
tracks above.

REFUSING TO HELP AN NPC
- Minor: small commonfolk/honor dip, NPC remembers (their status_flags
  gets an "estranged" tag) but doesn't become hostile.
- Major (refusing a Signature NPC's core arc, e.g. leaving Corwin Ashe's
  sister search unresolved): permanent relationship lock — NPC never
  offers their full arc again this playthrough.

KILLING AN NPC
- Background NPC: small infamy increase, localized commonfolk dip (only
  NPCs/locations connected to them react).
- Signature NPC: major infamy increase, that NPC permanently moves to
  world_flags-tracked "deceased_npcs" (mirroring DeceasedHero's
  structure) — other NPCs who knew them react in later scenes, and their
  role (ally/mentor/etc slot) may go permanently unfilled for the rest
  of the run, a real cost.
- Killing in self-defense vs. cold-blooded: gate the SEVERITY (not
  whether it counts at all) on HOW the kill happened — track this via a
  choice-specific effects flag like "reputation.honor: -2" (mercy
  available) vs "-8" (execution/ambush) on functionally similar combat
  outcomes.

BETRAYING AN NPC (promised help, then didn't / sold out their secret /
switched sides)
- Distinct from refusing — betrayal requires having FIRST agreed to
  help. Bigger honor/infamy swing than a simple refusal, and uniquely
  poisons npc.<id>.trust even for OTHER NPCs who hear about it (a
  "word travels" mechanic — betraying one Signature NPC should visibly
  cost trust with NPCs in their same social circle, e.g. betraying
  Guildmistress Yeva Solt costs standing with Petra Voss too, even
  though Petra is technically her rival).

NOT HELPING DURING A WORLD EVENT
- Track separately from individual NPC interactions —
  reputation.commonfolk and reputation.crown both take a hit if the
  player is repeatedly absent/inactive during major stages of War/
  Rebellion/Plague events, scaled to how many "intervention points"
  (section 3.2) were skipped.
- This should NOT be a single binary flag — "helped in 2 of 5
  intervention points" should read narratively as partial, inconsistent
  involvement, not full credit or full blame.

SIDING WITH A FACTION/NATION
- Every faction-siding choice should write to BOTH the faction's own
  approval AND at least one other faction's disapproval — sided with
  the Northern Reach in the war event should cost reputation.crown
  specifically, not just grant a generic "you chose a side" flag.
- Recommend a THIRD tracked value per major conflict beyond the four
  global ones for exactly this purpose, e.g.
  world_flags.player_northern_standing, scoped to that one war rather
  than bloating the four global trackers with dozens of one-off
  factions.

ADDITIONAL TRIGGERS WORTH TRACKING (the "broader" ask):
- BREAKING A PROMISE (distinct from betrayal — a promise made casually
  and simply forgotten/deprioritized, smaller honor cost than active
  betrayal, but still tracked, since RDR-style systems reward promises
  MATTERING at all, even small ones).
- THEFT — stealing from a Signature NPC vs a background one should scale
  the same way killing does; stealing from a Merchant NPC specifically
  should affect that merchant's prices/willingness to sell rare goods
  going forward, a very legible, low-cost-to-implement consequence.
- MERCY TO AN ENEMY — sparing Thane Dolgrym Ashveil or Inquisitor Rane,
  for instance, rather than killing them, should be trackable as its own
  positive honor trigger distinct from "didn't kill someone" being the
  mere absence of the negative one — mercy should read as an active,
  rewarded choice, not just a non-penalty.
- EXPLOITING A VULNERABLE NPC (e.g. using the Plague event's scarce
  medicine to extort rather than help) — a sharp infamy/honor split:
  high infamy, low honor, distinct signature from straightforward
  villainy, since it's coldly transactional rather than violent.
- KEEPING A SECRET YOU WERE TRUSTED WITH — a positive-only trigger, no
  real mechanical cost to breaking it in many cases, so this is where
  you reward RESTRAINT specifically — small trust gains with the NPC
  whose secret it was, invisible to everyone else, modeling that not
  every good choice needs to be publicly legible to matter.
- PUBLIC VS PRIVATE ACTIONS — the same choice (e.g. an execution) should
  have a bigger reputation swing if it happens in front of witnesses
  (town square) vs privately (an alley) — recommend a lightweight
  "witnessed: true/false" tag on relevant SceneChoice.effects blocks
  rather than a whole new system, since it's really just a multiplier on
  the existing triggers above.

4.3 SEVERITY TIERS (concrete "what actually changes")
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
MINOR consequences (single-digit reputation shifts):
- An NPC's dialogue tone shifts (warmer/colder greeting text via a
  narrativeVariants entry keyed to the relevant flag/reputation range).
- Prices shift slightly at affected merchants.
- A single new dialogue option appears/disappears next time you meet
  that NPC.

MODERATE consequences:
- An NPC's role can shift (an ally becomes a neutral wildcard; a rival
  becomes a genuine enemy).
- A whole location's background-NPC population mood shifts (village
  described as "wary of outsiders" in narrativeVariants text going
  forward).
- Certain merchants refuse to sell you specific (not all) goods.

MAJOR consequences:
- Permanent NPC death or permanent relationship lock (arc closes for
  the rest of the run).
- A world event's trigger conditions/stage timing shift (rebellion
  starts earlier, war outcome range narrows).
- Certain ENDINGS become locked in or locked OUT entirely — see section
  5 (Narrative Routes) for how routes gate on accumulated reputation.
- Legacy Engine impact: extreme reputation states at time of death
  should feed into the DeceasedHero record itself — e.g. a
  majorCanonEvents entry like "Ruled through fear, not law" for a
  high-infamy/low-honor death, giving FUTURE playthroughs' NPCs
  something specific to react to (an NPC could say "I remember what the
  last one who wore that face was like" if infamy was extreme).

5. NARRATIVE ROUTES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
Routes are not separate branches of the story tree (remember: this
project deliberately uses the State-Driven Web approach, not a hardcoded
exponential tree — see the original build plan). A Route is a PATTERN of
accumulated reputation/flags/NPC relationships that the story recognizes
and reacts to, mostly through which ENDING becomes available and how
late-game NPCs treat the player — not a hard fork early in the story.

LOVE ROUTE
- Built from sustained npc.<id>.trust with a Rival or Ally-role NPC
  specifically (Cassian Thorne, Sarel Windholt, Petra Voss, and Delphine
  Corvassa are all strong candidates given their existing rivalry/ally
  framing).
- Mid-route beats: choosing that NPC's wellbeing over a mission objective
  at least once; defending them publicly during a World Event.
- Endings: mutual partnership ending (varies dramatically by which NPC —
  a Vampire romance ending reads very differently than a Civilian one);
  a tragic ending if the NPC dies to a World Event the player failed to
  intervene in; a rejection ending if trust was pursued too
  transactionally (tracked via the "exploiting a vulnerable NPC" trigger
  crossing over into this NPC specifically).

REDEMPTION ROUTE
- Built from LOW starting reputation (either the player made early
  villainous choices, OR inherited infamy from a prior playthrough's
  DeceasedHero) combined with a sustained pattern of mercy/honor
  triggers reversing the trend.
- Mid-route beats: explicitly rejecting a Corruption-route opportunity;
  a Mentor NPC (Bramwell, Ancient Voss, Elowen) directly acknowledging
  the change.
- Endings: full redemption (high honor by end, NPCs explicitly forgive);
  partial redemption (honor recovered, but a specific Signature NPC never
  forgives — a bittersweet, and often more interesting, outcome);
  failed redemption (reversion under pressure during a late World Event).

REVENGE ROUTE
- Built from a specific triggering wrong (an NPC's betrayal, a World
  Event casualty) tracked as a dedicated flag, then a sustained pattern
  of choices prioritizing that target over other objectives.
- Mid-route beats: passing up unrelated opportunities to stay focused on
  the target; possible collateral damage to OTHER NPC relationships as a
  visible cost of tunnel vision.
- Endings: hollow victory (target defeated, honor/infamy read as
  "empty" via a specific majorCanonEvents phrasing); redirected ending
  (revenge target is spared/redirected into the Redemption route
  instead, for either party); consuming ending (revenge succeeds but
  costs the player their own Signature relationships along the way).

POWER / CONQUEST ROUTE
- Strongest for Royal Heir origin, viable for others via the
  Succession Crisis or War events.
- Mid-route beats: choosing crown reputation over commonfolk repeatedly;
  using Quartermaster Cray's double-dealing rather than exposing it;
  following Dowager Regent Elowen's pragmatic-but-cold advice literally.
- Endings: consolidated rule (high crown, low commonfolk — a stable but
  feared reign); benevolent rule (power gained without commonfolk
  reputation collapsing — hardest ending to reach, requires balancing
  both trackers); overthrown (power seized without enough crown-track
  support to hold it).

SACRIFICE ROUTE
- Not origin-locked; built from a pattern of choices that cost the
  player PERSONALLY (gold, an item, a relationship, eventually
  potentially the character's own life via a flagged fatal choice) to
  benefit others/the world state.
- Mid-route beats: repeatedly choosing World Event intervention options
  that carry a personal cost tag over safer alternatives.
- Endings: the character's death itself can BE the ending here — a
  isFatal choice taken willingly in a final World Event, with a
  majorCanonEvents entry reflecting the sacrifice specifically, meant to
  read as a triumphant rather than tragic DeceasedHero entry — the
  Legacy Engine should let future NPCs speak of a sacrifice hero with
  reverence, distinctly from other causes of death.

ISOLATION / EXILE ROUTE
- Built from a pattern of REFUSING Ally/Mentor relationships, staying
  transactional with Merchants only, and low engagement with World
  Events.
- Mid-route beats: explicitly turning down Mentor guidance more than
  once; no Signature NPC reaching meaningful trust by mid-game.
- Endings: the "wanders on" ending — genuinely open-ended, low
  reputation swings either direction, an intentionally quiet, low-drama
  conclusion that some players will specifically be pursuing; a "found
  too late" tragic variant if isolation coincides with a World Event
  the player could have prevented with allies they didn't have.

CORRUPTION / FALL ROUTE
- Built from following ethically-compromised Mentor advice (Elowen,
  Ancient Voss's rejected lessons) combined with rising infamy and
  falling honor simultaneously.
- Mid-route beats: exploiting vulnerable NPCs during World Events;
  betraying a Signature Ally specifically for material/political gain.
- Endings: total corruption (an antagonist-coded ending, but still
  playable to its own conclusion, not a game over); a last-minute pull-
  back into Redemption if a very specific late-game mercy choice is
  taken; a "consumed by it" ending where the character becomes a FUTURE
  world event's antagonist — literally feeding a new DeceasedHero-based
  Undead Uprising or similar event for the next playthrough.

FAITH / ZEALOTRY ROUTE
- Strongest tie-in to the Sun Temple Schism event; built from consistent
  religious/ideological choice patterns (siding with new_orthodoxy or
  zealot_uprising stages specifically).
- Mid-route beats: prioritizing the Schism's resolution over personal
  relationships; Inquisitor Rane content is the clearest through-line
  here regardless of the player's own origin.
- Endings: founder (a new, lasting religious institution -
  majorCanonEvents-worthy on its own); martyr (a sacrifice-adjacent
  fatal ending specific to this route's themes); disillusioned (the
  route's natural "lost the faith along the way" conclusion, which
  doesn't have to be tragic — can transition cleanly into Isolation).

DIPLOMACY / UNITY ROUTE
- The "hardest to fail into accidentally, hardest to achieve on purpose"
  route by design — requires ACTIVELY balancing multiple faction
  reputations rather than just avoiding conflict.
- Mid-route beats: successfully using the Foreign Invasion event's unify
  -other-factions mechanic (section 3.2); brokering the
  stalemate_treaty / concessions_won branches of War/Rebellion rather
  than letting them resolve by force.
- Endings: a genuine "best outcome for the most people" ending, meant to
  be the hardest one to reach in the whole route list, since it requires
  succeeding across MULTIPLE systems (Honor, World Events, and several
  NPC relationships) simultaneously rather than committing hard to one
  axis the way every other route does.

6. HOW THIS ALL CONNECTS — A WORKED MINI-EXAMPLE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
To make the abstract systems above concrete, here's how one small arc
touches every system in this document at once:

A Civilian-origin player befriends Mira Talbot (Signature Ally) early.
Her Redemption-flavored subplot (she's stealing from a neighbor) resolves
with the player's help — small honor gain, npc.mira_talbot.trust rises.
Years later, the Harvest Failure World Event triggers. Mira's farm is
one of the affected households. Because of the earlier relationship, a
UNIQUE scene becomes available (gated on npc.mira_talbot.trust >= N)
letting the player personally intervene for her specifically, beyond the
event's generic intervention points. Helping her costs the player
resources they needed elsewhere (a Sacrifice Route beat). Word spreads
(reputation.commonfolk rises visibly). Later, in a Succession Crisis
scene, a background NPC (built from the generation framework: core trait
#7 "apologizes before disagreeing," motivation #13 "earning a place in a
community that hasn't fully accepted them") recognizes the player's name
specifically because of the commonfolk reputation gain, and offers help
they wouldn't have otherwise — a background NPC's behavior changed
because of a Signature NPC interaction three "chapters" earlier, which
is the whole point: nothing in this system is meant to work in
isolation.

7. AUGUST 2026 EXPANSION — ORIGIN STARTING STORIES, CASCADING WORLD
   EVENTS, EXPANDED NPC ROSTER, RANDOMNESS, AND SCOPE NOTES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
This section was added in response to a round-trip design pass done in
draw.io (two flowcharts: an origin-branching skeleton and a marked-up

7.1 ORIGIN STARTING STORIES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
Today, only 3 of the 7 origins get anything unique in the prologue
(Royal Heir and Vampire get extra choices at the caravan fork; Civilian
and Royal get reworded text at prologue_start / meet_mira). Wanderer,
Elf, Dwarf, and Mercenary currently see identical generic text
everywhere. This closes that gap: every origin gets its own OPENING beat
and one origin-specific HOOK (a complication or choice) before all paths
rejoin the shared prologue_ruin_encounter -> Chapter 1 flow. Cheapest
version to build: these are TEXT + CHOICE variants layered onto the
existing prologue_start/prologue_village scenes (same pattern Royal/
Vampire already use), not new scene files — same engine, no new code.

  WANDERER — Opening: no ceremony memory, no farm to leave; wakes at a
  cold campsite along the road, the King's-hero story just another tale
  heard secondhand from a fellow traveler. Hook: finds an abandoned
  camp with someone else's gear and a half-finished letter. Choice:
  take the gear and move on (practical, small Gold gain) vs. try to
  find whoever left it (costs time, opens an early Ally/Wildcard hook
  and a small reputation.commonfolk gain).

  CIVILIAN — Opening: the morning of leaving the family farm, same day
  as the ceremony memory. Hook: a parent or sibling presses the Family
  Heirloom Ring into their hand with a condition attached — "only sell
  it if you're truly desperate." Choice: promise to keep it (sets a
  flag the game can call back to later if the player ever does pawn it)
  vs. stay noncommittal (no flag, no guilt, no promise to break).

  ROYAL HEIR — Opening: slipping out of the castle unseen, as already
  established. Hook: a guard on the postern gate recognizes the signet.
  Choice: bribe them to silence (Gold cost), invoke Aldric authority to
  order silence (free, but the guard resents it — small crown/commonfolk
  tradeoff), or confide the real reason for leaving (opens an early
  Ally hook with that specific guard).

  ELF — Opening: one more long road, indistinguishable in memory from a
  hundred before it. Hook: a villager mistakes them for someone who
  passed through generations ago — a debt, a promise, or a wrong the
  Elf genuinely remembers and the villager only knows as folklore.
  Choice: correct them (reveal true age/nature — a small trust
  interaction) or let the mistake stand (keeps them anonymous, small
  missed-connection cost).

  DWARF — Opening: leaving the mountain halls, a handshake and nothing
  more as goodbye. Hook: a clan debt-collector (or rival clan member)
  catches up before they've cleared the foothills, testing whether the
  Dwarf's word is still good outside the mountain. Choice: pay the debt
  (Gold cost), work it off on the spot (a Strength/Constitution check,
  delays departure), or refuse outright (starts the run with a hostile
  NPC relationship already in place).

  VAMPIRE — Opening: hiding hunger and curse in plain sight, as already
  established. Hook: the first real test of the hunger — an easy,
  unnoticed target (a drunk alone on the road) presents itself before
  the player has even reached the first village. Choice: resist (no
  mechanical cost, but the game notes it — first data point for a
  Redemption-flavored route) vs. feed (small Constitution/health
  benefit, but reputation.commonfolk takes an early, permanent hit if
  it's ever discovered later).

  EXILED MERCENARY — Opening: hunted by the old company, already
  established. Hook: a former comrade catches up first — not to kill
  them, necessarily, just to talk, warn, or size them up. Choice:
  fight (starts the run already bloodied), flee (costs Gold or gear
  left behind), or hear them out (opens a recurring rival/ally hook
  tied to the old company's eventual reappearance, per 2.3's existing
  Mercenary Signature NPC content).

  CANDIDATE NEW ORIGINS (open question, NOT yet decided — flagged from
  the "new origin (think another)" nodes in the draw.io draft). Adding
  an 8th+ origin means also touching archetypes.dart, stat bonuses,
  starting gear, and eventually content parity across every system in
  this document — pick at most one or two, don't add all of these:
    - Beastkin/Therian — raised at the edge of the Hollow Reaches
    - Reformed Cultist — ties naturally into the Sun Temple Schism event
    - Court Mage's Apprentice — academic arcane path, distinct from the
      Elf's "old magic" flavor
    - Plague-Orphan — survivor of an earlier Plague World Event
    - Star-Reader — omen-touched, ties into the Comet/Omen Year event
  Recommendation: don't commit to any of these until the 7 existing
  origins actually have written scenes behind their new hooks above —
  adding an 8th origin before that is scope-creep on top of scope-creep.

7.2 CASCADING WORLD EVENTS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
The existing World Event Catalog (3.2) treats each event as mostly
independent. This adds a CHAINING layer on top — events that resolve
badly can trigger a bigger event, so an ignored small crisis becomes a
much bigger one, and the world keeps moving even when the player is
focused elsewhere. Mechanically: each WorldEvent gains an optional
`cascadeTriggers` map of {finalOutcome: nextEventId}, checked whenever
an event resolves. This is additive to the existing WorldEvent/Stages
model in world_events.dart — no structural rewrite needed.

Two new catalog entries, to support the cascade the draw.io draft
sketched (war / dragons / dark legion / rebellion / apocalypse / demon
lord):

  --- THE DARK LEGION ---
  Trigger: cascades from EITHER "War with the Northern Reach" resolving
  as northern_victory/stalemate_treaty (a weakened, war-torn realm looks
  like easy prey) OR "The Rebellion of the Salt Road" resolving as
  regime_change/crushed with high infamy (a fractured realm invites
  outside opportunists). Can also trigger standalone late-game.
  Stages: omens -> incursion -> occupation -> (repelled / bargained_with
  / realm_falls).
  Player intervention: unifying surviving factions from whichever prior
  event(s) triggered this — the highest payoff for players who kept
  multiple threads alive instead of "winning" any single earlier event
  outright.
  NPC impact: can permanently remove Signature NPCs who were left weak
  or exposed by the triggering event; strongest emotional stakes in the
  cascade specifically because the player will recognize why it happened.
  Legacy tie-in: a past playthrough that died during War or Rebellion
  markedly raises the odds this event fires at all in the next run.

  --- THE DEMON LORD (optional endgame antagonist) ---
  Trigger: cascades from "The Dark Legion" resolving as realm_falls, OR
  from "The Sun Temple Schism" resolving as zealot_uprising with the
  Dark Legion already active (two unrelated origins' content converging
  into the worst-case ending).
  Stages: revealed -> gathering_dread -> confrontation -> (the runs's
  final ending state — this is designed as a possible CAPSTONE, not a
  routine mid-game event).
  Player intervention: the realm's last-resort event — deliberately
  rare and hard to reach by design, so it stays meaningful.
  NPC impact/Legacy tie-in: whichever Signature NPCs survived every
  prior cascade step are the ones who show up here — this event is
  built entirely out of the wreckage/survivors of everything before it,
  not new content of its own.

"Apocalypse" from the draft is intentionally NOT a separate catalog
entry — it's the WORST possible outcome label across the whole cascade
(Dark Legion -> realm_falls, with Demon Lord unresolved), used in
Chronicle/History screen text rather than as its own event. Keeps the
cascade legible instead of adding an event that means the same thing as
"everything else failed."

7.4 RANDOMNESS & PROCEDURAL VARIANCE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
Ties into the existing DiceRoller (1d20 + modifier vs DC) rather than
adding a second random system:

- SCENE TWISTS — a lightweight table (weather, an overheard rumor, a
  minor NPC's mood) rolled per scene visit to vary flavor text without
  touching plot state. Cheap, high atmosphere value, never blocks or
  changes a choice's outcome.
- WORLD EVENT TRIGGER ROLLS — events with a time-based trigger (Plague,
  Foreign Invasion) roll a weighted chance per in-game year rather than
  firing on a fixed year, so two playthroughs with identical choices
  can still diverge. Weights shift based on WorldHistory state (a
  fractured realm = higher roll weight for bad events), keeping
  randomness meaningful rather than pure noise.
- BACKGROUND NPC ASSEMBLY — every time a scene calls for an unnamed
  NPC, roll Tables 2.4.A-D + 7.3.A-B instead of hand-picking, so the
  world doesn't feel authored one villager at a time.
- LEGACY-WEIGHTED RANDOMNESS — rolls aren't uniform; a past
  DeceasedHero's causeOfDeath and majorCanonEvents nudge relevant
  weights (per the Legacy tie-ins already documented throughout section
  3.2) — "random" here means "random within a world that remembers,"
  not random in a vacuum.

7.5 SCOPE, SCALE, AND INFLUENCES — a grounded note
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
Worth saying plainly, since "even if it takes 1GB" came up: this is a
text-and-branching-choice game. Every scene, every NPC entry, the whole
World Event catalog — all of it is structured text/JSON. A genuinely
vast version of this game (hundreds of scenes, 200+ named NPCs, a dozen
cascading world events, full Narrative Route coverage) would likely
total a few megabytes of actual game data, not gigabytes — storage size
was never really the bottleneck here, WRITING TIME is. That's not a
reason to shrink the ambition, just a reason to measure "vastness" in
scene count / NPC count / hours of branching content, not disk space —
illustrations and audio (both currently unimplemented, per game.txt's
"What's NOT implemented yet") are the only pieces of this project that
would ever meaningfully move the storage needle.

On "use many books as reference": the strongest, safest way to do this
is naming TONAL/STRUCTURAL touchstones rather than pulling specific
plot beats or prose from any one source — e.g. the gamebook tradition
(Fighting Fantasy, Choose Your Own Adventure) for the branching-choice
structure already in place; tabletop RPG lineage (the D&D-style 6-stat
system already adopted in archetypes.dart) for character build feel;
epic/political fantasy generally (multi-faction succession crises,
world events that don't wait for the player) for the World Events
system's tone. Naming influences like this is fine; reproducing text,
named characters, or specific plots from any copyrighted book is not,
and isn't necessary — everything in this document is already original
to this project.

8. THE WIDER WORLD — CONTINENTS, KINGDOMS, AND THE FOUR PEOPLES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
Fair callout: everything through section 7 was really one country
(Aldric's Realm) with a few off-map hints (Northern Reach, Salt Sea).

four major peoples (Human/Elf/Dwarf/Orc), the Dark Legion's own

8.1 WORLD GEOGRAPHY (six landmasses around a central sea)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
- THE HEARTLANDS (human continent) — Aldric's Realm is the largest
  kingdom here, but not the only one. A second human kingdom, THE
  MARCH OF VESSAIR, sits on the same continent — rival, not enemy by
  default; centuries of an uneasy border along the Greywater river.
  Vessair is ruled by a Merchant-Council rather than a monarchy, giving
  Civilian/Mercenary-origin players a second political flavor distinct
  from Aldric's court intrigue.
- AELENDOR (elf continent, northwest across the Salt Sea) — home
  continent of the Elves; the Silverleaf Wood (already on the existing
  map) is a single enclave of Aelendor-born Elves living abroad in
  Aldric's Realm, not the whole nation. Aelendor itself is ruled by the
  CONCLAVE OF THE UNBROKEN BOUGH, a rotating council of the oldest
  living Elves — "rotating" on a timescale of centuries, which is its
  own dark joke to a long-lived people.
- KHAZGARD (dwarf continent, south, mountainous/subterranean) — the
  Deep Halls on the existing map are one clanhold; Khazgard has SEVEN
  Great Clanholds total, loosely federated under the HIGH FORGE COUNCIL,
  which meets only when a threat is big enough to require it (most of
  the time, clans handle their own business — matches the existing
  Dwarf archetype's "a handshake means more than a smile" flavor).
- THE ASHREACH (orc continent/lands, southeast) — NOT a single horde;
  three major Orc nations with real political differences: the
  IRONJAW COALITION (settled, agrarian, the nation most willing to
  trade with humans), the SKARR HOST (semi-nomadic, warrior-honor
  culture, the nation most likely to be flattened into "just raiders"
  by outsiders who've never met one), and the ASH-BOUND (a smaller,
  isolationist nation built around a form of ancestor-worship tied to
  volcanic sites). Deliberately three different political cultures, not
  one "orc kingdom," for the same reason Aldric's Realm isn't the only
  human nation.
- THE BLIGHTED EXPANSE (Dark Legion's home continent, far south) —
  where the Dark Legion (section 7.2) actually comes FROM when it
  cascades into the Heartlands; not a kingdom in any normal sense, more
  a corrupted territory that used to be something else (deliberately
  left vague — a good hook for a future "what was this place before"
  Elf/Dwarf lore quest, since both peoples are old enough to remember).
- THE ABYSSAL RIFT (Demon continent) — doesn't sit on the normal map at
  all; a landmass that only fully "arrives" (becomes reachable/visible)
  once the Demon Lord event (7.2) is active. Before that, it's rumor,
  omens, and the Comet/Omen Year event's flavor text — keeps it feeling
  like an genuine endgame reveal rather than a location the player could
  wander into early and break pacing.

8.2 POWERFUL MAGES & INDEPENDENT FORCES (answer to no crown)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
- THE CONVOCATION OF AETH — the closest thing the world has to a
  supreme magical authority; a neutral order (accepts Human, Elf, Dwarf,
  and Orc members alike) headquartered on a floating spire-city above
  the central sea, visible from all six continents on a clear day
  (great "everyone looks up" world-flavor beat). Officially neutral in
  every political conflict in this document; unofficially, individual
  Convocation mages absolutely take sides, which is where most mage-NPC
  content should live rather than "the Convocation" as a monolith.
- THE SUNKEN CHOIR — a heretical offshoot of the old Sun Temple faith
  (ties directly to world_flags.sun_temple_destroyed and the Sun Temple
  Schism event, 3.2) that believes the temple's destruction was
  necessary, not tragic — a small, dangerous, ideologically coherent
  antagonist faction, distinct from the Dark Legion (political/
  religious threat vs. existential/military one).
- THE FREE COMPANIES — not one org, the umbrella term for independent
  mercenary companies (including the player's old company, if
  Mercenary-origin) who work for whichever of the above kingdoms pays
  best; the natural home for cross-nation Mercenary content since they
  aren't bound to any single kingdom's politics.
- LOOSE THREAD (intentional): who or what founded the Convocation, and
  why it's neutral by CHOICE rather than by weakness, is left open — a
  good candidate for a late-game Elf/Dwarf lore quest given both
  peoples are old enough to have been there.

8.3 A NOTE ON ORCS AS A PLAYABLE ORIGIN
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
Since Orcs now have real political weight in the world (8.1), add ORC to
the candidate-new-origins list from 7.1 — and specifically, an origin
tied to the IRONJAW COALITION (the nation most likely to actually leave
home and travel among humans) reads as the most natural fourth-race
playable origin if/when a new origin actually gets built, ahead of the
five origin candidates already listed in 7.1. Still not committed — same
"don't add an origin before the existing 7 have their new hooks written"
caveat from 7.1 applies.

8.4.A WHY THE LAST TWO LISTS ARE SHORT (design note, not an oversight)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
The Dark Legion and Demon continents get deliberately FEWER named
characters than everywhere else. An antagonist faction with 8 named,
fully-personified members reads as "a kingdom, but evil" — it flattens
the threat. Keeping these two mostly title/role-based (the Herald, the
Quiet Ones) and reserving named specificity for LEGACY CALLBACKS
(corrupted echoes of NPCs the player already knew) keeps the existential
threats feeling different in kind from the political ones, not just
reskinned.
